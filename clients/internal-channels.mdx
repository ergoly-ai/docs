Before a worker talks to Acme directly, give it a channel where your team can see what it would say. That channel is **internal**, because only your people are in it, but the work in it is about one client.

## Setting it up {#setup}

1. Create the channel in Slack with your own people in it.
2. Invite the worker. When it asks what the channel is for, press **Internal channel**, not the client.
3. Ask the worker for the client's work there: *draft Acme's weekly status*. Your team's copy answers, with Acme's folder in reach, so the draft is about Acme.
4. When the drafts are right, invite the worker to the channel Acme is in, and set that one up for Acme.

## Why it is internal {#why}

Because your team's copy answers there, it knows the whole company: every client, every connection placed with the company. That is what you want backstage. In Acme's own channel, only Acme's copy answers, which knows less, on purpose. See [Client separation](/clients/separation).

<Note>
  **Worth knowing.** Keep client contacts out of the internal channel. The channel decides who is answered, so a guest in an internal channel would be answered by your team's copy.
</Note>
