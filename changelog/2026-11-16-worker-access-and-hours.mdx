This week you can see who may use a worker and who may change it, your admins can see the hours your workers are using, and a worker whose Slack connection dies comes back on its own.

## Who can use a worker, and who can change it

Every worker now carries two rights, Use and Edit, each set to a level on your organization chart and checked in code — in Slack, in control and on the API. Existing workers let everyone in your organization use them and employees change them; a new worker lets only a person's superiors on the organization chart change it.

## Worker hours you can see

Every turn now records the worker hours it used, and your organization's admins can see the hours used by worker, client and task.

## Improvements

- **A worker whose Slack connection dies pauses itself** — A worker whose Slack connection stays dead is paused, and comes back on its own, instead of retrying a dead connection forever.
- **Unused email domains are released** — An organization's email domain is claimed only once email is turned on, and a domain that is never used is released. A retry now waits at most an hour instead of six.

## Fixes

- **Files to connected tools work** — A worker can hand a file to a connected tool, and a file error is no longer reported as the tool refusing the credential.
- **A task made from a busy DM keeps its requester** — When several people message a worker at once, the task it creates records who asked, and reports back to them.
- **A scheduled task can post into a DM thread** — A task a worker schedules from a direct message can now post back into that thread.
- **A task scheduled into a client's channel runs** — A task a worker schedules from a direct message into a client's channel now runs.
- **A reply that breaks midway fails sooner** — A round whose reply stream breaks partway through no longer hangs for thirteen minutes before it fails.
