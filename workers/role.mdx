A worker's **role** is its job title and what the job is for: *Account Manager*, *SEO Analyst*, *Bookkeeper*. You set the title when you create the worker and can change it under Settings → Identity. The title is what people see under its name, and it is the first thing the worker reads about itself.

## One worker, one role {#one-role}

While there's no right answer for how to architect your company and its workers, we think the question of how many workers to run is a question about roles, not tasks. If two jobs would be done by two different people at your agency, incentivized and judged by two different standards, make two workers. If one person would do both, make one worker and give it two skills.

Not sure where to start? Try just one. When it starts making mistakes (responding slowly, forgetting things, or mixing up incentives) it might be time to convert a worker into a whole team.

Here's some more thoughts on it: [staff it, don't wire it](https://www.ergoly.ai/thoughts/staff-it-dont-wire-it).

## Persona {#persona}

From the name, the title and the company, Ergoly writes the worker's persona: who it is, how it introduces itself, whose name it works under. The persona is rewritten whenever the worker is rebuilt, so it always matches what control says. The parts you control are the [responsibilities](/workers/responsibilities) and [behaviors](/workers/behaviors).

<Note>
  **Worth knowing.** Give the title the way you would on a job posting. A worker with a non-standard or fun title may work for people at your company—but AI workers might ask more questions or be more creative in its responses than one with a standard title.
</Note>
