A worker is a named member of your Slack workspace: its own app, its own avatar, in the channels you invite it to. Four kinds of place, four pages:

- **[Internal channels](/workers/channels/slack/internal-channels)**: Your team's own channels. The worker answers from everything it knows, every client included.
- **[Client channels](/workers/channels/slack/client-channels)**: A channel that belongs to one client. The worker answers from that client's world and nothing else.
- **[DMs](/workers/channels/slack/dms)**: A direct message. Who you are decides which copy answers.
- **[Group DMs](/workers/channels/slack/group-dms)**: A message to the worker and a few people at once.

## When a worker joins a channel {#joining}

Invite a worker to a channel the way you invite a person. It stays only if a workspace admin invited it, and it asks the inviter, privately, what the channel is for, with a suggestion.

If no client matches, it offers **New client…**, which creates the client and sets the channel up in one go. If only your team is in the channel, it suggests **Internal channel**. **Leave channel** backs out.

## Threads and progress {#threads}

In a channel, a worker always replies in a thread. While it works, its Slack status shows *is thinking…*. A message that arrives while it is busy gets a 👀 reaction and is handled next; several messages from the same person in a row are handled as one.
