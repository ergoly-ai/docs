A worker on its own works for your team. To put it in front of a client, you need a **client** in Ergoly and a Slack channel that belongs to that client. The AI Admin can set both for you when asked.

<SlackScene
  scene={{"channel":"Ada","kind":"dm","lines":[{"from":"you","name":"Sam","text":"Add Acme as a client and put Nora in #acme. Daniel Reyes is their contact."},{"from":"worker","name":"Ada","text":"I'll create the client Acme, assign Nora to it, set #acme as Acme's channel, and bind Daniel Reyes to Acme so his DMs with Nora go to Acme. Nora will join #acme itself. OK?"},{"from":"you","name":"Sam","text":"OK."},{"from":"worker","name":"Ada","text":"Done. Acme is live in #acme. Nora is there now, and only sees Acme's world when it is working for Acme."}]}}
  channels={[]}
/>

In this example, three key things happened. A client called Acme now exists. Nora has a separate copy for Acme, with Acme's memory and nothing else. And #acme is mapped to Acme, so a message there always reaches that copy.

## Invite the worker yourself {#invite}

You can also invite a worker to a channel in Slack, the way you would invite a person. It asks you, and only you, what the channel is for. See [When a worker joins a channel](/workers/channels/slack).

<Note>
  **Worth knowing.** Start with an internal channel for the client, where your team reviews the worker's drafts, before inviting it into the channel the client is in. See [Internal client channels](/clients/internal-channels).
</Note>
